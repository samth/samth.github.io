Due: Thursday, September 10, 2026, at 11:59 PM.

Points: 10.

Submission: A text entry in Canvas.

Download, compile, and run GCC 16.2.0, available from the GCC 16.2.0 release directory. Use your agent to overcome difficulties and things you do not understand about the process.

Submit a paragraph that you wrote yourself, not AI-generated text, explaining how the process went and what you had to do to succeed. Submit through the Build gcc assignment in Canvas.

3.2 Assignment Structure🔗

The expected assignment structure for the rest of the course is:

  • Process notes. Students will keep a paper lab notebook, not a computer-only notebook. Bring the notebook to class and write down plans, sketches, questions, decisions, stuck points, and observations while working. In addition to the paper notebook, students will keep programmatic records such as commits, branches, transcripts, logs, test output, and notes on what changed.

  • Short builds. Early assignments will ask students to use coding agents to build small, testable artifacts under specific constraints.

  • Review exercises. Students will inspect generated code, identify defects and risks, and improve test suites or evaluation harnesses.

  • Final project. Students will design, build, evaluate, and present a larger project that uses agents as programming collaborators or as part of the resulting software system.

All enrolled students should use Codex through IU’s ChatGPT Edu workspace with their @iu.edu email address. Use your IU email address for course work; do not use a personal account or API-key login unless an assignment explicitly says to do so. See Codex Access for setup instructions.

Detailed grading weights, submission instructions, and collaboration rules will be posted before assignments are released.
